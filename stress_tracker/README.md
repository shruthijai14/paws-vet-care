# Vet Visit Stress Tracker

## Overview
The Vet Visit Stress Tracker is a pilot module designed to identify observable signs of stress in animals prior to or during a veterinary visit. It provides a structured, non-diagnostic way to capture owner-reported and optional staff-observed behaviors in order to support Fear Free handling, safer workflows, and improved communication between caregivers and veterinary teams.

This module serves as the foundational component of the broader AI-Assisted Veterinary Care Workflow.

---

## What This Module Does
- Collects observable, behavior-based stress indicators using a short pre-visit questionnaire
- Converts qualitative observations into a simple quantitative stress score
- Categorizes stress into low, moderate, or high tiers
- Provides handling considerations aligned with Fear Free principles
- Establishes a baseline stress profile that can be referenced across future visits and post-operative recovery

This tool is intended to support preparation and communication, not to diagnose anxiety, pain, or behavioral disorders.

---

## Tools Used
- **Google Forms** for data collection from pet owners and optional staff observations
- **Google Sheets** for automated scoring, tier classification, and visual organization

This approach was selected to reflect realistic clinic workflows and to prioritize accessibility, ease of use, and rapid piloting without disrupting existing systems.

---

## Why This Approach
Veterinary stress is often recognized informally but inconsistently documented. By using simple, standardized inputs and transparent scoring logic, this module makes stress visible and actionable early in the visit process.

The use of low-code tools ensures:
- Minimal training requirements
- Ease of iteration based on clinical feedback
- Ethical transparency and interpretability
- Feasibility for small clinics without dedicated technical infrastructure

---

## Scope & Ethics
This module:
- Does not diagnose medical or behavioral conditions
- Does not replace veterinary judgment
- Does not make autonomous decisions
- Is intended solely as a decision-support and communication aid

All constraints are intentional to ensure ethical use, clinical safety, and appropriate scope for a student-led pilot project.
